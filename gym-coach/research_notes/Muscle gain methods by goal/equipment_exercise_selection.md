# Exercise selection for muscle growth with limited equipment, and an equipment-to-exercise mapping (as of November 2026)

Scope: adult recreational lifters, training mostly alone at home, muscle size first and strength second.

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[TRIAL]** = randomized or within-subject training study with a size or strength outcome
- **[META]** = meta-analysis or systematic review
- **[EMG]** = acute muscle-activation study (does not measure growth)
- **[CASE/SURVEILLANCE]** = injury case report or injury-database analysis
- **[COACHING]** = coaching practice or evidence-synthesis writing, not trial evidence

Access caveat for the report writer: in this session pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and www.strongerbyscience.com could not be opened directly (blocked by the session's network settings). Every number below was taken from search-engine summaries of abstracts and repository records, not from full texts read end to end. Where a number came only from a secondary summary (blog, news write-up) it is flagged "secondary". Nothing here was recalled from memory without a source; items I could not source are in the Gaps lists.

## 1. Free weights vs machines for hypertrophy and strength

### Takeaway
Two meta-analyses agree that equipment type does not change muscle growth when training is otherwise comparable; strength gains are specific to the device you test on. For a home app this means "no machines" is not a hypertrophy handicap in itself.

### Cited Findings
- [META] Haugen et al. 2023 (BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation) pooled 13 studies and 1,016 participants (strength n = 12 studies, jump n = 5, hypertrophy n = 5), interventions of at least 6 weeks — [Haugen et al. 2023, PMC10426227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- [META] Hypertrophy did not differ between free weights and machines: SMD -0.055, p = 0.751 — [Haugen et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- [META] Strength measured with free-weight tests rose more after free-weight training (SMD -0.210, 95% CI -0.391 to -0.029, p = 0.023); strength on machine tests tended to rise more after machine training; direct comparisons of dynamic and isometric strength showed no significant difference — [Haugen et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- [META] Authors' conclusion: choose by individual preference and by the strength test you care about; they "speculate that the combination could result in more complete hypertrophy" (speculation, not a tested result) — [Haugen et al. 2023, DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a7282dba5e674176bde2a8239832d2f7)
- [META, via secondary summaries] Heidel, Novak & Dankel 2022, "Machines and free weight exercises: a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing changes in muscle size, strength, and power": muscle size, power and device-neutral strength gains were similar; free-weight training won on free-weight tests and machine training won on machine tests — [Biolayne REPS summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-16/free-weights-vs-machines-for-muscle-gains/); also described in [Haugen et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- [COACHING] Stronger By Science commentary on Haugen notes the jump result was not clearly significant and that a sensitivity analysis (one study removed) favoured free weights for countermovement jump; treat as secondary — [Stronger By Science, free weights vs machines](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/free-weights-vs-machines/)

### Inferences
- Because the hypertrophy comparison is null and strength is test-specific, an app can treat a barbell, dumbbell, cable or machine version of the same movement pattern as interchangeable for muscle growth, and rank options by loadability, stability, safety when alone and user preference.
- A user who wants to get stronger at a specific lift (for example barbell bench) should train that lift; a user who only wants size does not need it.

### Gaps
- Heidel et al. 2022 primary record was not retrieved; journal name, number of included studies and effect sizes are unverified here (the assignment brief names the paper; searches returned only secondary summaries).
- Only 5 studies fed the Haugen hypertrophy estimate, so the null is not tightly bounded. I could not read the confidence interval for the hypertrophy SMD.
- No trial found comparing a full free-weight home programme with a full machine programme in trained lifters over more than a few months.

## 2. Bodyweight and band training (push-ups vs bench press, elastic vs conventional resistance, progression)

### Takeaway
Load-matched push-ups produced similar chest and triceps growth and similar bench strength gains to bench press in small, short trials; elastic resistance matches conventional resistance for strength in a meta-analysis, but that meta-analysis did not test hypertrophy. Bodyweight movements stay useful only if they are progressed (leverage, elevation, added band or load).

### Cited Findings
- [TRIAL] Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017 (J Exerc Sci Fit): 18 young men, 9 per group, 2 sessions per week for 8 weeks, bench press at 40% 1RM vs push-ups position-adjusted (for example kneeling) to the same load. Pectoralis major and triceps thickness increased in both groups; bench 1RM rose 60.0 to 65.0 kg (bench group) and 61.1 to 64.2 kg (push-up group), both p < 0.01. Biceps thickness increased only in the bench group (28.4 ± 3.3 to 31.5 ± 3.7 mm). Power and muscular endurance did not change — [Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017, PMC5812864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812864); [PubMed 29541130](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29541130/)
- [TRIAL] Calatayud et al. 2015 (J Strength Cond Res): 30 university students with advanced training experience; EMG matched between 6RM bench press and band-loaded 6RM push-up; 5 weeks of training; both groups improved 1RM and 6RM similarly, control did not change. Outcome was strength, not muscle size — summarised in [Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812864) and [search record of the paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Calatayud+bench+press+push-up+comparable+levels+of+muscle+activity)
- [TRIAL] Kotarsky et al. 2018 (J Strength Cond Res): progressive calisthenic push-up training (moving through harder variations) produced bench press 1RM gains similar to bench press training — [PubMed 29466268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/); thesis version at [NDSU repository](https://library.ndsu.edu/ir/handle/10365/28060?show=full)
- [Biomechanics] Ebben et al. 2011: ground reaction force is about 64% of body weight in a regular push-up, about 49% in a bent-knee push-up, about 41% with hands on a 61 cm box, and about 74% with feet on a 61 cm box; the authors propose using these to scale push-up load as a percentage of body mass — [Ebben et al., ISBS proceedings](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4457/4146)
- [META] Lopes et al. 2019 (SAGE Open Medicine): 8 randomized trials, elastic resistance vs weight machines or free weights, strength outcome only. No significant difference: corrected values are lower limb SMD -0.11 (95% CI -0.40 to 0.19, p = 0.48) and upper limb SMD 0.09 (95% CI -0.18 to 0.35, p = 0.52). The original text swapped the upper and lower limb figures; use the erratum — [Lopes et al. 2019, PMC6383082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082); [erratum, PMC7485002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7485002)
- [EMG] Bergquist et al. 2018 (J Hum Kinet): 29 participants, 10RM-matched flyes and reverse flyes with bands vs dumbbells. Bands gave slightly lower activity in the target muscles (pectoralis major, posterior deltoid) and substantially higher activity in ancillary muscles, so bands and dumbbells were not simply equivalent for single-joint fly patterns — [Bergquist et al. 2018](https://johk.pl/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/10078-61-2018-v61-2018-01.pdf)
- [EMG] Andersen et al. 2020 (Front Sports Act Living): 16 resistance-trained men, deadlift with free weights vs free weights plus bands; no significant differences in muscle activation between conditions — [Andersen et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2020.598284/text)
- [EMG, small] van den Tillaar 2019 concluded push-ups and bench press can be used interchangeably for strength training, in experienced resistance-trained men — cited within [search record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728153)

### Inferences
- The push-up evidence supports "push-ups are a real chest and triceps exercise if the load is high enough to bring the set close to failure in a sane rep range", not "a standard push-up equals a heavy bench press for a strong lifter". Kikuchi tested a 40% 1RM load in untrained men for 8 weeks.
- A practical push-up progression ladder for the app, ordered by the Ebben loading data: hands elevated, then knees, then regular, then feet elevated; beyond that add a band across the back, a loaded backpack, deficit handles for more stretch, or move to one-arm-biased variations. The first four rungs are measured; the rest is coaching practice.
- For bodyweight movements with no convenient leverage ladder (for example rows, single-leg squats), the generic progression tools are: more range of motion, slower lowering, pauses in the stretched position, unilateral versions, and added external load. These are coaching practice, not trial-tested as a ladder.
- Bands are a sound strength tool, but their resistance is lowest where the muscle is longest. Given the long-muscle-length findings in section 6, bands are probably the weakest tool for isolation work unless the user steps further from the anchor so there is tension at the start of the rep. This is an inference from combining Bergquist 2018 with the muscle-length trials; it has not been tested directly.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis found on elastic resistance vs conventional resistance for hypertrophy specifically. Lopes 2019 covers strength only.
- Calatayud 2015 did not measure muscle size, so it cannot be cited as hypertrophy evidence (the assignment brief groups it with hypertrophy claims).
- Whether Kotarsky 2018 found similar muscle thickness changes between groups could not be confirmed from the abstract summary.
- No long-term (over 6 months) trial of bodyweight-only training for hypertrophy in trained adults was found.
- No hypertrophy trial of pull-up, inverted-row or pistol-squat progressions was found.

## 3. Low-load training close to failure, and its practical limits

### Takeaway
Across a wide load range, sets taken to or near failure build similar muscle; heavy loads are better for 1RM strength. The practical floor is roughly "not as light as 20% 1RM", and very light sets to failure are longer and more uncomfortable, which matters when a user's dumbbells are light.

### Cited Findings
- [META] Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (J Strength Cond Res 31(12):3508-3523): 21 studies, low load (≤60% 1RM) vs high load (>60% 1RM), all sets to momentary failure, at least 6 weeks. 1RM strength favoured high loads; isometric strength did not differ; muscle size changes were similar. Conclusion: "muscle hypertrophy can be equally achieved across a spectrum of loading ranges" — [PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)
- [META] Lopez et al. 2021 (Med Sci Sports Exerc 53(6):1206-1216), network meta-analysis: 28 studies, 747 healthy adults, sets to volitional failure, low (>15RM) vs moderate (9-15RM) vs high (≤8RM) load. No significant hypertrophy difference between load categories. Strength: high vs low effect size about 0.60-0.63, moderate vs low about 0.34-0.35; high vs moderate not significant — [PubMed 33433148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/)
- [TRIAL] Lasevicius et al. 2018 (Eur J Sport Sci): within-subject, volume-matched 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% 1RM. All loads increased size and strength, but 20% 1RM was "suboptimal" for hypertrophy; 80% was best for strength — [Lasevicius et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2018.1450898); summary at [Examine](https://examine.com/faq/are-heavy-or-light-weights-better-for-muscle-gain)
- [TRIAL] Lasevicius et al. 2022: high-load and low-load training to concentric failure both increased quadriceps cross-sectional area by 7.7-8.1% after 8 weeks; low-load training stopped short of failure produced no statistically significant change — [search record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Lasevicius+muscle+failure+low-load+high-load+hypertrophy)
- [TRIAL] Fisher & Steele 2017 (Muscle Nerve 56(4):797-803): 7 men, unilateral knee extension to momentary failure at 80% vs 50% of maximum voluntary isometric torque. Strength gains were similar; the lighter condition meant more repetitions, longer sessions and higher discomfort. Authors: "discomfort should be considered before selecting a training load" — [Fisher & Steele 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.25537)

### Inferences
- With light dumbbells the app should not cap reps at a classic 8-12; it should let sets run to roughly the 15-30+ range and require the set to end close to failure. Lasevicius 2022 suggests that low-load sets stopped well short of failure are the main way light-load training fails.
- Once a movement needs far more than about 30 repetitions (the load is drifting toward the 20% 1RM zone that underperformed in Lasevicius 2018), the app should switch tactics instead of adding reps: unilateral versions, slower lowering, pauses at the stretched position, a longer-range variant, or a different exercise that is harder at the same load. The 30-rep trigger is my reading of the cited loads, not a number any single study defines.
- Expect lower adherence with long, painful sets (Fisher & Steele 2017). Light loads are best reserved for isolation and upper-body work; for legs, single-leg variants raise the relative load without more equipment.
- Users who care about 1RM strength need access to heavy loads at least some of the time; light-load-only programmes are a size tool first.

### Gaps
- The discomfort evidence retrieved is small (n = 7 in Fisher & Steele 2017). Ribeiro et al. 2019 (Int J Sports Med, "Acute Effects of Different Training Loads on Affective Responses in Resistance-trained Men") was identified as relevant but its results were not verified.
- No sourced upper repetition limit for hypertrophy was found; the "about 30% 1RM floor" often quoted in coaching material was not traced to a primary source in this session.
- How close to failure low-load sets must go (reps in reserve) was not quantified from the sources retrieved.

## 4. Home-based vs gym-based resistance training outcomes

### Takeaway
I found no meta-analysis directly comparing home-based and gym-based resistance training for hypertrophy in healthy young or middle-aged adults. The nearest evidence is in older adults and clinical groups, where supervised or centre-based training tends to give somewhat larger strength gains; the case that home training can match the gym rests on the equipment and load evidence in sections 1-3.

### Cited Findings
- [META] Costa et al. 2023 (J Aging Phys Act), older adults: home programmes improved physical function vs control; no difference from supervised programmes in gait, mobility and balance, with a trend toward supervised programmes for strength — [catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edb,168590355)
- [META] Mañas et al. 2021 (Ageing Res Rev), unsupervised home-based resistance training in older adults: significant lower-limb strength improvement, Hedges' g = 0.33; authors call the evidence modest and preliminary — [PubMed 34022464](https://www.pubmed.gov/34022464/)
- [META] Zhang et al. 2024 (Front Public Health), knee osteoarthritis, 11 trials: home-based exercise was as effective as centre-based exercise for short-term pain, disability, walking ability and lower-limb strength; evidence described as limited — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1360824/full)
- [META] A 2024 BMC Geriatrics review concluded unsupervised home-based exercise programmes "have little impact on lower extremity functions in older adults" — [BMC Geriatrics 2024](https://bmcgeriatr.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12877-024-05393-4.pdf)
- [TRIAL, preprint, not peer reviewed] 20 untrained premenopausal women: no significant differences in strength, muscle mass or power gains between a blended home/gym programme and a gym-only programme — [SportRxiv preprint 158](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/158)

### Inferences
- The setting itself is probably not the active ingredient. The plausible reasons home programmes underperform in older-adult trials are low loads, no progression and no supervision of effort, all of which an app can address with progression rules and effort targets. This is an inference; the reviews above do not isolate the cause.
- The app's main jobs at home are therefore the ones a gym or coach would otherwise supply: enforce progressive overload, cue proximity to failure, and pick exercises the user's equipment can load adequately.

### Gaps
- No direct home vs gym hypertrophy meta-analysis in healthy non-elderly adults was found. Secondary websites claim "ACSM-cited reviews show no difference when volume and overload are equated"; I could not trace those claims to a primary paper and have not used them.
- ACSM and NSCA primary guidance documents were not retrieved in this session.

## 5. Exercise variety, number of exercises per muscle, and compound-only vs compound plus isolation

### Takeaway
Some planned variety helps hit all regions of a muscle, but frequent or random rotation does not help and may hurt; one or two well-chosen exercises per muscle is a defensible default. Compound lifts alone under-train some muscles or heads (biceps, triceps long head, rectus femoris, hamstrings), so a home programme should add isolation work for those. The well-known "isolation adds nothing" studies from Barbalho and colleagues are compromised by retractions.

### Cited Findings
- [META/Systematic review] Kassiano et al. 2022 (J Strength Cond Res 36(6):1753-1762): 8 studies, 241 young men. "Some degree of systematic variation seems to enhance regional hypertrophic adaptations and maximize dynamic strength, whereas excessive, random variation may compromise muscular gains"; redundant exercises and very frequent swapping may hinder adaptation — [PubMed 35438660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/)
- [TRIAL] Fonseca et al. 2014 (J Strength Cond Res): four programmes varying exercises, intensity, or both. Groups that varied exercises gained more strength, and only the exercise-varying groups grew all four quadriceps heads (head-level detail is from secondary summaries and the author's commentary) — [Fonseca et al. 2014](https://www.doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000539); [full text PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Changes%20in%20exercises%20are%20more%20effective%20than%20in%20loading%20schemes%20to%20improve%20muscle%20strength.pdf)
- [TRIAL] Kassiano et al. 2024 (Res Q Exerc Sport): 70 young women, systematically varied vs non-varied exercise routines produced "virtually similar" strength gains and hypertrophy — [ECU repository record](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4912)
- [TRIAL] Gentil et al. 2013: adding single-joint exercises to a multi-joint programme gave no additional muscle size or strength gains in untrained young men — [Gentil et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1139/apnm-2012-0176)
- [TRIAL, integrity concerns] Barbalho and colleagues published several trials (trained women, untrained women, untrained men, trained men and bodybuilders) reporting no added benefit from single-joint exercises — examples at [Solent repository](https://pure.solent.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-adding-single-joint-exercise-to-a-multi-joint-re/) and [PMC6317138](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6317138)
- [Critique] Vigotsky and co-authors posted the white paper "Improbable data patterns in the work of Barbalho et al." (SportRxiv, July 2020), noting among other things that effects in the multi-joint group "nearly perfectly match" the single-plus-multi-joint group, and requested retraction of the remaining papers — [Stronger By Science, Barbalho](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/barbalho/); [Retraction Watch, July 2020](https://retractionwatch.com/2020/07/21/calling-exercise-data-atypical-improbable-and-to-put-it-bluntly-pretty-weird-sleuths-call-for-seven-retractions/)
- [Retractions] The European Journal of Sport Science retracted the single-joint bodybuilder paper after external statistical review; a 2020 J Strength Cond Res paper on single-joint exercise in untrained young women was also retracted; further retractions followed — [EJSS retraction notice](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17461391.2022.2116178); [Retraction Watch, December 2021](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/12/17/exercise-researcher-earns-more-retractions-as-investigations-mount/); [PubMed retraction record 32804467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32804467/)
- [TRIAL] Mannarino et al. 2021: within-subject, 10 participants, 8 weeks, one arm dumbbell row and the other biceps curl. Elbow flexor thickness rose 11.06% with curls vs 5.16% with rows — [Mannarino et al. 2021 PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Single-Joint%20Exercise%20Results%20in%20Higher%20Hypertrophy%20of%20Elbow%20Flexors%20Than%20Multijoint%20Exercise.pdf)
- [TRIAL] Brandão et al. 2020 (J Strength Cond Res): 43 young men, bench press only vs lying triceps extension only vs both in either order. Pectoralis major CSA grew more in all bench-containing groups than triceps-extension only. Total triceps CSA increased significantly in every group except bench-only. Triceps long head grew more in groups that included the triceps extension than bench-only; the lateral head grew more with the multi-joint protocols — [PubMed 32149887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/)
- [TRIAL] Kubo et al. 2019 (Eur J Appl Physiol): squat training (full or half) did not change rectus femoris or hamstring volume — [Kubo et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)
- [TRIAL, secondary summary] Zabaleta-Korta et al. 2021: leg extension grew the rectus femoris more than Smith machine squats at proximal, middle and distal sites; the Smith squat grew distal vastus lateralis more — [Biolayne REPS summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-45/do-compound-lifts-really-do-it-all-squats-vs-leg-extensions/)

### Inferences
- Default rule for the app: one primary exercise per movement pattern, plus a second exercise for a muscle only when it covers a region or head the first one misses (examples: add a curl to rows and pull-ups; add an overhead or lying triceps extension to presses; add a leg extension or equivalent to squats for rectus femoris; add a hamstring exercise to squats).
- Keep exercises stable for several weeks so progression can be tracked; rotate on a planned basis or when an exercise stalls or hurts. Random session-to-session swapping is the pattern the Kassiano 2022 review warns against.
- Do not cite the Barbalho single-joint studies as evidence that isolation work is unnecessary. The unretracted Gentil 2013 result applies to untrained men over a short period and is contradicted for specific muscles by Mannarino 2021 and Brandão 2020.
- The number of distinct exercises needed per muscle is not established by trials; "one to two, chosen to cover different regions or lengths" is my synthesis of the findings above.

### Gaps
- No trial directly tests how many different exercises per muscle group are needed.
- Costa et al. 2021 (varied vs fixed exercise selection in trained men), Baz-Valle et al. 2019 and Rauch et al. 2020 (auto-regulated exercise selection) are relevant trials I looked for but did not retrieve; their results are not reported here.
- A meta-analysis of single-joint vs multi-joint training for hypertrophy was not retrieved, and any such pooled estimate published before the retractions may include Barbalho data.
- The current retraction status of each individual Barbalho paper was not checked one by one.

## 6. Best-supported exercises per muscle group, including muscle length / stretch-position evidence

### Takeaway
The clearest muscle-specific findings are that training a muscle at longer lengths tends to grow it more: overhead triceps extensions, seated (hip-flexed) leg curls and stretched-position calf raises all beat their short-length counterparts in direct trials. Evidence that one implement beats another (cable vs dumbbell lateral raise, squat vs hip thrust for glutes) is mostly null, and the pooled range-of-motion data do not show a reliable advantage for lengthened partials over full range.

### Cited Findings

**Triceps**
- [TRIAL] Maeo et al. (Eur J Sport Sci 2023;23(7):1240-1250, online 2022): 21 adults, 12 weeks, one arm doing elbow extensions overhead and the other with the arm at the side (neutral). Triceps hypertrophy was "substantially greater" overhead despite lower absolute loads, most clearly in the long head — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)
- [Secondary figure] Long-head volume gain reported as 28.5% overhead vs 19.6% neutral in a secondary summary; verify against the paper before quoting — [sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/)
- Caveat: the study compared arm position (overhead vs neutral) on a cable, not a branded "pushdown vs overhead extension" contest, and an earlier study (Stasinaki et al. 2018) found no significant difference between positions — [sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/)
- [TRIAL] Bench press alone did not significantly increase total triceps CSA and under-stimulated the long head relative to lying triceps extensions — [Brandão et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/)

**Hamstrings**
- [TRIAL] Maeo et al. 2021 (Med Sci Sports Exerc 53(4):825-837): 20 adults, 12 weeks, 70% 1RM, 5 sets of 10, 2 sessions per week, seated leg curl with one leg and prone with the other. Whole-hamstring volume +14% seated vs +9% prone; biarticular muscles +8-24% vs +4-19%; the monoarticular muscle did not differ (+10% vs +9%) — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [PMC7969179](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179)
- [TRIAL] Bourne et al. 2017 (Br J Sports Med): 30 recreationally active men, 10 weeks, hip-extension exercise vs Nordic hamstring exercise vs control (10 per group). Biceps femoris long head volume increased more with hip extension than Nordics (d = 1.03, p = 0.037); semitendinosus volume increased with both, with no significant difference — [ACU repository record](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q534/impact-of-the-nordic-hamstring-and-hip-extension-exercises-on-hamstring-architecture-and-morphology-implications-for-injury-prevention)
- [EMG/fMRI] The same group reported that hip-extension exercise selectively activates the long hamstrings and the Nordic preferentially recruits semitendinosus — [University of South Wales record](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/en/publications/impact-of-exercise-selection-on-hamstring-muscle-activation/)
- [TRIAL] Squats do not grow hamstrings: no hamstring volume change after full or half squat training — [Kubo et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y); hamstrings showed little to no growth after either squat or hip thrust training — [Plotkin et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

**Calves**
- [TRIAL] Kassiano et al. 2023 (J Strength Cond Res 37(9):1746-1753): 42 young women, 8 weeks, 3 days per week, calf raises through full range vs initial partial (stretched half) vs final partial (top half). Medial gastrocnemius growth was greatest with initial partials — [PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)
- [Secondary figures] About 15.2% (initial partial) vs 6.7% (full range) vs 3.4% (final partial) for medial gastrocnemius thickness; lateral head differences were less clear — [BarBend write-up](https://barbend.com/train-past-failure-for-the-thickest-calves)
- [TRIAL, 2025] In resistance-trained people, adding past-failure partials in the stretched position gave a 0.62 mm greater increase in medial gastrocnemius thickness than full-range training alone — [BarBend write-up](https://barbend.com/train-past-failure-for-the-thickest-calves)

**Biceps / elbow flexors**
- [TRIAL] Kassiano et al. 2025 (Int J Sports Med): 63 untrained young women, 8 weeks, 2 sessions per week, preacher curl (n = 30) vs incline curl (n = 33). Incline curl increased proximal elbow flexor thickness more; preacher curl increased distal thickness more; strength gains were exercise-specific. Ultrasound did not separate biceps from brachialis — [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/); [ECU repository record](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/5817)
- [TRIAL] Curls beat rows for elbow flexor growth (11.06% vs 5.16%) — [Mannarino et al. 2021](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Single-Joint%20Exercise%20Results%20in%20Higher%20Hypertrophy%20of%20Elbow%20Flexors%20Than%20Multijoint%20Exercise.pdf)
- [TRIAL] Low-load bench press, but not low-load push-ups, increased biceps thickness in Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017 (an incidental finding; pressing is not a biceps exercise) — [PMC5812864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812864)

**Deltoids (lateral)**
- [TRIAL] Larsen et al. 2025 (Front Physiol): 24 participants, 8 weeks, 2 sessions per week, one arm cable lateral raise and the other dumbbell lateral raise. Lateral deltoid thickness rose 3.3-4.6%; Bayesian analysis gave "moderate" support for no difference at distal and proximal sites and "extreme" support for the null in the multivariate analysis, despite the cable loading the muscle more at long length — [Larsen et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468/full)

**Chest**
- [TRIAL] Chaves et al. 2020 (Int J Exerc Sci 13(6):859-872): 47 untrained men, horizontal bench (n = 15) vs incline bench (n = 15) vs both (n = 17). Thickness change at the upper pectoralis site (second intercostal space) was greatest with incline, 0.62 cm more than horizontal (95% CI 0.23 to 1.0, p = 0.003); strength changes were similar across groups — [Chaves et al. 2020](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol13/iss6/12); [PMC7579505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505)
- [TRIAL] Push-ups at a matched load grew pectoralis major similarly to bench press — [Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812864)
- [TRIAL] Bench press (alone or with triceps extensions) grew pectoralis major more than triceps extensions alone — [Brandão et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/)

**Quadriceps**
- [TRIAL] Squats grow the vasti but not the rectus femoris — [Kubo et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)
- [TRIAL, secondary summary] Leg extensions grew rectus femoris more than Smith squats at all three measured sites — [Biolayne REPS summary of Zabaleta-Korta et al. 2021](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-45/do-compound-lifts-really-do-it-all-squats-vs-leg-extensions/)
- [TRIAL] Squat training grew quadriceps and adductors more than hip thrust training — [Plotkin et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- [TRIAL] Rear-foot-elevated split squats and back squats both improved strength, sprint and agility over 5 weeks in academy rugby players, with no significant between-group difference (strength and performance outcomes, not muscle size) — [Speirs et al. 2016 PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Unilateral%20vs.%20Bilateral%20Squat%20Training%20for%20Strength,%20Sprints,%20and%20Agility%20in%20Academy%20Rugby%20Players.pdf)

**Glutes**
- [TRIAL] Plotkin et al. 2023 (Front Physiol): untrained college-aged participants, 9 weeks, hip thrust vs back squat. Gluteal hypertrophy was similar between groups; gluteus medius and minimus showed little to no growth in either — [Plotkin et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- [TRIAL] Full squats increased gluteus maximus volume by 6.7 ± 3.5% and adductor volume by 6.2 ± 2.6%, more than half squats — [Kubo et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)

**Lats / back**
- [EMG, weak] A pull-up variation study (about 1.5 times biacromial grip width) found no significant differences in latissimus dorsi, biceps or posterior deltoid activity between variations — [PMC5548150](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5548150)
- [EMG, secondary] Reports of pulldown studies: narrow, medium and wide pronated grips gave broadly similar lat activation in 15 trained men (2014); a pronated grip beat a supinated grip while width did not clearly matter (Lusk et al. 2010) — [EliteFTS summary](https://elitefts.com/blogs/training/wide-or-narrow-grip-pull-downspull-ups-does-it-make-a-difference-needs-formatted)
- [TRIAL] Rows alone gave about half the elbow flexor growth of curls, so rows and pull-ups should not be counted as full biceps work — [Mannarino et al. 2021](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Single-Joint%20Exercise%20Results%20in%20Higher%20Hypertrophy%20of%20Elbow%20Flexors%20Than%20Multijoint%20Exercise.pdf)

**Abs**
- [TRIAL] A 9-week study in resistance-trained women comparing "upper" (supra-umbilical) and "lower" (infra-umbilical) abdominal exercises concluded that exercise choice "does not generate regionally different hypertrophy responses" in the rectus abdominis (the URL below is the most likely match among the search results; attribution not fully confirmed) — [search result record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10908688)
- [Acute ultrasound/EMG] Rectus abdominis shortening and thickening occurred in all exercises tested and was most pronounced in the crunch — [PMC10824285](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10824285/)

**Range of motion and muscle length, pooled**
- [META] Wolf et al. 2023 (Int J Strength Cond): full vs partial range of motion, trivial SMD 0.12 (95% CI -0.02 to 0.26) in favour of full range. A subgroup of only three studies suggested a possible benefit of partials at long muscle lengths (SMD -0.28, 95% CI -0.81 to 0.16), with an interval crossing zero — [Wolf et al. 2023, DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/99e86b317bec40f5abc2f0008358737e)
- [Systematic review] Kassiano et al. 2023, "Which ROMs Lead to Rome?", reviews range-of-motion effects on hypertrophy — [PubMed 36662126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662126/)

### Inferences
- A safe ranking rule for the app: among exercises the user can do, prefer the one that loads the target muscle through a full range and is hard in the stretched position, then break ties by loadability and comfort. Do not hard-code "lengthened partials beat full range"; the pooled evidence (Wolf 2023) does not support that as a general rule.
- Triceps: every programme should include an overhead or lying extension, since presses under-train the long head (Brandão 2020, Maeo 2023). This is available with a single dumbbell, a band, a kettlebell or a cable.
- Hamstrings: knee flexion with the hip flexed (seated leg curl) is the best-supported option but needs a machine or a cable/band workaround. Hip-extension work (Romanian deadlift, 45-degree back extension) and Nordics appear complementary, emphasising biceps femoris long head and semitendinosus respectively (Bourne 2017).
- Calves: do calf raises off a step so the heel can drop into a full stretch, pause there, and extend sets with stretched-position partials. Both studies used machines; applying this to single-leg bodyweight or dumbbell raises is an inference.
- Biceps: incline and preacher curls grew different regions (Kassiano 2025), so neither is simply "best"; an incline curl (needs an adjustable bench) plus one curl with a harder shortened or mid-range position is a reasonable pair. The claim that incline curls are superior overall is not supported by the one direct trial found.
- Lateral delts: implement does not matter much (Larsen 2025). Dumbbells are sufficient; cables are optional.
- Glutes: squats and hip thrusts are interchangeable for gluteus maximus in novices (Plotkin 2023); deeper squats do more for glutes and adductors (Kubo 2019). Neither grew gluteus medius/minimus.
- Lats: no longitudinal hypertrophy trial was found comparing pull-ups, pulldowns and rows. Recommending pull-ups/chin-ups and rows as the default is coaching practice supported only by weak EMG data.

### Gaps
- Exact triceps head-by-head percentages from Maeo 2023 and the exact calf figures from Kassiano 2023 come from secondary summaries; verify against full texts.
- Kinoshita, Maeo et al. 2023 (Front Physiol, standing vs seated calf raise; [likely URL](https://frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/pdf)) surfaced in search but was not opened; its result is not reported here.
- Pedrosa et al. 2022 (leg extension initial vs final partial range) and Varovic et al. 2024 (meta-analysis of mean muscle length and regional hypertrophy) were not retrieved.
- No hypertrophy trial found for lats, traps, rear delts or abs comparing specific exercises; no ab wheel or hanging leg raise data were retrieved (Escamilla et al. 2010 EMG paper not found in this session).
- No hypertrophy trial found for band-only or kettlebell-only isolation exercises.
- Most of these trials used untrained participants for 8-12 weeks; several used only women or only men.

## 7. Substitution table: movement pattern by equipment set

### Takeaway
Every major muscle group can be trained with at least one reasonable option in every equipment set, but three are genuinely hard without specific gear: lats (need a bar or a secure anchor), hamstrings at meaningful load (need weight, a band, or a way to anchor the feet), and heavy lower-body loading in general when only bodyweight or bands are available. The table itself is coaching practice built on the trial findings in sections 1-6; it is not itself trial evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Equipment type does not change hypertrophy when training is otherwise comparable, which is what licenses substitution across columns — [Haugen et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- Push-ups can stand in for bench press when load is matched — [Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812864); push-up variation loads range from about 41% to about 74% of body weight — [Ebben et al.](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4457/4146)
- Elastic resistance produces strength gains comparable to conventional resistance — [Lopes et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082); [erratum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7485002)
- Light loads work if sets go close to failure, with 20% 1RM as a suboptimal floor — [Lopez et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/); [Lasevicius et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2018.1450898)
- Split squats matched back squats for strength and performance over 5 weeks — [Speirs et al. 2016](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Unilateral%20vs.%20Bilateral%20Squat%20Training%20for%20Strength,%20Sprints,%20and%20Agility%20in%20Academy%20Rugby%20Players.pdf)
- Squats and hip thrusts do not grow hamstrings; hip-extension exercise and Nordics do — [Kubo et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y); [Plotkin et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full); [Bourne et al. 2017](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q534/impact-of-the-nordic-hamstring-and-hip-extension-exercises-on-hamstring-architecture-and-morphology-implications-for-injury-prevention)
- Kettlebell research is thin: scoping reviews rate confidence as low to very low, with typically trivial to small effect sizes, and I found no hypertrophy-specific review — [Meigh et al. 2019 scoping review, DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/034dc129d99845a09c115bf925f577e1); [2014 literature review record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4031772)

**Muscle groups that are hard to train well without specific equipment, with the best workaround [COACHING]**
- Lats with no pull-up bar and no anchor: this is the single biggest hole in a bodyweight-only or dumbbell-only setup. Best fixes in order: buy a doorway pull-up bar; use a band with a door anchor for pulldowns and straight-arm pulldowns; rows (one-arm dumbbell, inverted row under a table) cover much of the back but not loaded shoulder adduction overhead; dumbbell pullovers are a partial stand-in. The app should flag this gap and recommend a bar.
- Hamstrings without load: squats, lunges and hip thrusts will not do it (Kubo 2019; Plotkin 2023). Bodyweight options that do load them are Nordic curls (feet anchored), sliding leg curls and single-leg hip-extension work. Nordics are very hard for beginners, so the app needs a regression path (band-assisted, partial range, eccentric-only).
- Seated (hip-flexed) leg curl effect without a machine: a seated band or cable leg curl is the closest home approximation of the long-length advantage in Maeo 2021. This transfer is an inference; the trial used a machine.
- Heavy lower-body loading with bands or bodyweight only: move to single-leg work early (split squats, step-ups, pistol progressions) and use deep range and pauses; accept higher reps to failure per section 3.
- Lateral delts with bodyweight only: essentially no loaded option. Any light object (water jug, loaded bag) works given the low loads involved; presses and pike push-ups hit front delts more than lateral delts.
- Rectus femoris without a leg extension: squats will not grow it (Kubo 2019). Reverse Nordics and sissy squats are the bodyweight substitutes; neither was trial-tested in the sources retrieved.
- Upper chest without an adjustable bench: feet-elevated push-ups or low-to-high band presses approximate an incline; the incline advantage itself comes from one trial (Chaves 2020).
- Kettlebell-only users: one fixed weight means progression must come from reps, unilateral work and harder variations rather than load; this collides with the low-load limits in section 3 for big lower-body lifts sooner than for upper-body or isolation work.

**Selection rules an app could implement [COACHING synthesis of sections 1-6]**
1. For each pattern, pick the leftmost available option the user can load heavily enough that a set reaches near failure inside the repetition range discussed in section 3; fall through to the next equipment column otherwise.
2. Always schedule, in addition to the compound patterns: a curl, an overhead or lying triceps extension, a hamstring exercise (hinge and, when possible, knee flexion), a lateral raise, a calf raise and, when available, a rectus femoris exercise.
3. Prefer variants with a hard stretched position and full range; do not rotate exercises more often than every several weeks unless pain or a stall forces it.
4. If any required pattern has no adequate option (most often vertical pull), surface an equipment recommendation instead of silently substituting a poor exercise.
5. Tag barbell bench press and barbell back squat as "requires rack with safeties when alone" and substitute dumbbell or single-leg versions when no safeties are present (see section 8).

### Gaps
- The table is not trial-validated as a whole; most cells have no direct hypertrophy study. Only the "evidence anchor" column points to trials.
- No sourced data on how well band or kettlebell versions of isolation exercises grow muscle.
- Vertical push, horizontal pull, vertical pull and abs have no direct hypertrophy trial behind the exercise choices listed.
- No evidence retrieved on dips specifically (growth or shoulder tolerance).
- Renaissance Periodization, Menno Henselmans and MASS substitution lists were not opened in this session, so the coaching column reflects general practice plus the trials above, not those specific guides.

## 8. Minimal high-value home-gym equipment, and safety when lifting alone

### Takeaway
Commercial home-gym guides converge on the same core: adjustable dumbbells plus an adjustable bench as the minimum, then a power rack with safeties, barbell and plates, with a pull-up bar and bands as cheap gap-fillers. The documented fatal risk for solo lifters is the barbell bench press pinning the neck or chest, so the app should require a rack with correctly set safeties for barbell bench (and squat) or default to dumbbells and push-ups.

### Cited Findings

**Equipment (all [COACHING]; sources are retailer or affiliate-style guides, prices are theirs and date quickly). Attribution caveat: these points came from a search summary covering several guides at once, so the link beside each point is the most likely source, not a confirmed one; re-check before quoting a price or a guide by name.**
- Typical core list, in priority order in one guide: power rack, barbell, weight plates, adjustable bench, adjustable dumbbells; a rack, barbell and bench together cover squats, presses, rows and deadlifts — [Legion Athletics guide](https://legionathletics.com/best-home-gym-equipment/); [REP Fitness guide](https://ca.repfitness.com/blogs/guides/best-home-gym-equipment-for-beginners)
- Minimal version: "a decent set of adjustable dumbbells and a weight bench can be your whole home gym for a long time"; several guides rate adjustable dumbbells the best value and suggest a set reaching at least 50 lb — [RitFit minimum equipment guide](https://www.ritfitsports.com/blogs/article/minimum-home-gym-equipment); [Antranik guide](https://antranik.org/the-only-home-gym-equipment-youll-ever-need-from-minimalist-to-ultimate-setups/)
- Example prices quoted in those guides: rack about $700 to $795 (REP PR-4000, Rogue RML-3W); adjustable dumbbells about $429 (Bowflex SelectTech 552) — [Legion Athletics guide](https://legionathletics.com/best-home-gym-equipment/); [Garage-gym equipment guide](https://www.tworepcave.com/1862/selecting-equipment-for-a-home-or-garage-gym/)
- Space estimates: a rack, bench and barbell fit in about 10 x 10 feet — [REP Fitness guide](https://ca.repfitness.com/blogs/guides/best-home-gym-equipment-for-beginners)

**Safety**
- [SURVEILLANCE, via case report] CPSC data: 20 weight-training fatalities from 1999 to 2002, at least 14 from asphyxiation and 12 tied to bench pressing; an estimated 3,820 bench press injuries in 2002, with 53% to the face or neck and 42% to the chest — [A Case of Fatal Asphyxia by a Barbell during a Bench Press, MDPI Forensic Sciences 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-6756/2/1/1)
- [CASE] Mechanism: fatigue lets the bar settle on the neck or chest, causing death by compression of the airway, neck vessels or breathing muscles; such deaths are described as rare but preventable — [Traumatic asphyxia in weightlifters, NCJRS abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/traumatic-asphyxia-weightlifters)
- [CASE] In the 2022 case report the safety bars were set at bench height and the lifter still died, so safeties only protect if set above neck and chest height at the bottom of the lift — [MDPI Forensic Sciences 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-6756/2/1/1)
- [SURVEILLANCE] A 2014-2023 NEISS analysis of 3,105 barbell injuries found lacerations were more strongly associated with barbell lifting at home than at recreational or sports facilities (injuries, not deaths) — [PMC11573732](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11573732)
- [Regulatory] In 2003 a petition asked the CPSC to require warning labels on bench press benches to prevent asphyxia deaths — [CPSC Federal Register notice (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081106031711/https://www.cpsc.gov/businfo/frnotices/fr03/weightlift.html)
- [COACHING] Never bench alone without a power rack with safety arms or pins; set them slightly below where the bar touches the chest — [Art of Manliness, 4 rules of bench pressing without a spotter](https://www.artofmanliness.com/strength/the-4-rules-of-bench-pressing-without-a-spotter/)
- [COACHING] Without a power rack, do not bench press alone; the risk is much greater — [Barbell Logic, Complete Guide to Barbell Safety](https://barbell-logic.com/?p=2716)
- [COACHING] Squat safeties: set about a fist-width, or an inch or two, below the bar's lowest point in the squat — [RitFit solo lifting guide](https://www.ritfitsports.com/blogs/article/solo-lifting-safety-master-power-rack-setup); [Starting Strength, importance of safeties](https://mail.startingstrength.com/article/the-importance-of-using-safeties-in-the-squat-and-bench-press)
- [COACHING, disputed] Collars: one source says collars should never be used when benching alone without spotter arms or pins (so plates can be tipped off); Starting Strength notes that leaving collars off might let you slide plates off in an emergency but does not address dropping the bar; a certification study guide says collars should always be used — [Art of Manliness](https://www.artofmanliness.com/strength/the-4-rules-of-bench-pressing-without-a-spotter/); [Starting Strength](https://mail.startingstrength.com/article/the-importance-of-using-safeties-in-the-squat-and-bench-press); [CSCS study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cscs/exercise-technique/spotting-equipment-safety)

### Inferences
- Coverage per dollar, mapped to the table in section 7 [COACHING synthesis]: (1) adjustable dumbbells plus adjustable bench cover every pattern except vertical pull; (2) a doorway pull-up bar closes the lat gap cheaply; (3) bands with a door anchor add pulldowns, leg curls, pushdowns and assistance for pull-ups; (4) a rack with safeties, barbell and plates is the upgrade that matters once dumbbells are outgrown for squats, hinges and presses, and it is the only setup in which barbell bench and squat are appropriate alone; (5) a kettlebell and dip station add little coverage beyond those. No source ranked these by cost per exercise, so the ordering is my synthesis.
- App safety rules that follow from the evidence: do not programme barbell bench press unless the user confirms a rack with safeties or spotter arms; show a one-time setup check that the safeties are high enough to keep the bar off the neck and face when the lifter lies flat (the fatal case had them at bench height); prefer dumbbell presses and push-ups for sets taken to failure; for squats require safeties or use goblet and single-leg variants; treat "no collars" as a last-resort measure for people without safeties, not a substitute for them.
- Sets taken close to failure are central to the hypertrophy case for light loads (section 3), so exercises where failure is safe alone (dumbbells, push-ups, pull-ups, split squats, bands) deserve priority in a solo home programme.

### Gaps
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning and ACSM guidance were not retrieved; the NSCA spotting rules (for example which lifts require a spotter) are not quoted here. The only NSCA-adjacent item found was a third-party CSCS study guide.
- No current annual count of bench press deaths was found; a figure of 10-12 US deaths per year appears in an opinion article and could not be verified, so it is not used.
- The CPSC figures are from 1999-2002 and were read via a case report, not from the CPSC source documents.
- No evidence-synthesis source (Stronger By Science, Barbell Medicine, MASS) was retrieved for equipment recommendations; the equipment findings rest on retailer and affiliate guides with an interest in selling equipment.
- No injury-rate comparison of dumbbell vs barbell pressing when training alone was found.
